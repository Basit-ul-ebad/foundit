Foundit

Clickable UI prototype for the campus lost-and-found app: 12 screens and the admin section. All names, items and scores are sample data for design review.

Three colours, one job each

Foundit uses only three colour families. Every other shade on screen is a lighter or darker tint of one of them, including the greys, which are tints of Ink. There is no red, green or purple. Meaning is also written in words (Lost, Found, Matched), so colour is never the only signal.

Teal #0A6B66
Found and trusted

Where: brand mark, main buttons, Found tags, Matched and Approved states, active tab.

Why: teal is the "safe, go ahead" colour. It marks the good outcomes in the flow: an item found, a match made, a claim approved. It sits between blue (trust) and green (success).

White text on teal 6.4:1. Teal text on its tint 5.5:1.
Amber #8F4A03 on #FDF1D9
Lost and needs attention

Where: Lost tags, the "I lost something" button, warnings (ID-photo warning, day-25 renew prompt), Pending states.

Why: lost-property tags and luggage labels are traditionally manila or amber, so users already link this colour to lost things. It reads as "pay attention" without the alarm of red.

Amber text on its tint 6.0:1. On white 6.7:1.
Ink #142030
Structure and text

Where: all text, icons, borders, page and card greys (as tints), the Delete button outline, selected chips.

Why: a deep navy-black keeps text sharp for reading in daylight on campus and gives the two colour roles room to stand out. It is the neutral the other two sit on.

Ink on white 16.4:1. Ink on both tints above 14:1.

How it represents the app

The app has two kinds of report, Lost and Found, and one goal: bring them together. Amber is Lost, Teal is Found, and Ink is the calm structure between. A user can tell a report type from across the screen, before reading a word.

Why it is not a default AI look

No purple-to-blue gradient, no cream and terracotta, no neon on black. The colours come from the subject: lost-property tags (amber) and a "found, safe" signal (teal). The logo is a baggage tag. Each colour has one fixed meaning instead of decoration.

Why it suits the target market

Hypothesis to test, not a result: students aged 13 and up on one campus want a calm, trustworthy app they can use in seconds, outdoors, on mid-range phones. Two clear colours, high contrast and plain words support that. We will check it in the usability test with 5 to 8 students (NFR-06).

Rules we follow

Only these three families. Text contrast at least 4.5:1 (NFR-07). Colour never works alone: tags carry words. Dark mode uses lighter versions of the same three.

Android and iOS from one Flutter codebase. This prototype is a design review tool; the real app will be built in Flutter. Match scores use the brief's starting weights and are illustrative, not validated results.
